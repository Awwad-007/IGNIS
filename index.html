The Six‑Inch Test

NASA certifies cabin materials by burning a strip on Earth and measuring six inches. Gravity changes how fire behaves, and the Moon has a sixth of it. You sign off the lunar cabin. Then we light it.

Drag the dial to see what gravity does to one flame. Five rounds follow: set the air, test the materials, send them to the Moon, then catch the two fires your instruments miss.

2

Burn the test strips

Standard test: ignite a strip on Earth, still air. If the flame climbs past six inches, the material fails. Pass means it goes in the cabin kit.

3

Send it to the Moon

Everything you approved goes to a cabin at lunar gravity, with your ventilation running. Same air, same material. Only gravity changed. A certified cabin scores its bends protection plus 8 points for every material you could keep. One hidden fire scores zero.

Will the alarm hear it?

BONUS

With no buoyancy, smoke particles linger and clump into bigger aggregates. A detector built around Earth-sized particles can stay silent while a cabin fills with smoke. Retune it until the alarm sounds in orbit.
